## Platform

web

## Users

A PRASA serves both people who live in São Vicente / Cabo Verde and people who are visiting, without treating either group as merely secondary.

People may arrive looking for practical information, opportunities to work or learn, local culture and things to do, places worth knowing, organizations, community initiatives, or ways to participate and give back.

The product should support both intentional search and useful discovery: someone may come looking for one thing and encounter something valuable they did not know to search for.

## Product Purpose

A PRASA is an independent digital public square rooted in Cabo Verde.

It brings together useful information, opportunities, local knowledge, culture, places, organizations, and ways to participate that are otherwise scattered across institutional websites, social media, WhatsApp, word of mouth, individual providers, and other sources.

A PRASA is intentionally curated rather than exhaustive. Its purpose is to make useful connections easier to discover, understand, and act on while pointing people toward the people and organizations behind the information.

Success is not becoming the largest directory. Success is becoming a calm, useful place to start — and a place people return to because it helps them find useful next steps.

## Positioning

A PRASA is a curated digital public square rather than a conventional link directory, official information portal, tourism site, or commercial marketplace.

Its differentiator is not completeness or official authority. It is intentional curation, clearer structure, source-led information, cross-category discovery, and surfacing useful next steps that people may not have known to look for.

A PRASA can bring together information that would otherwise require navigating multiple unrelated systems and social channels while preserving the identity and authority of the original provider.

## Operating Context

A PRASA began in Cabo Verde, with the current public experience centered on São Vicente / Mindelo.

The current site brings different kinds of useful information into one editorial experience, including:

- learning, work, tools, and opportunities;
- events, activities, and local culture;
- organizations and ways to participate or help;
- practical local knowledge and Mindelo Essentials;
- places and services worth knowing.

Listings ordinarily direct visitors to the provider or original source for the actual action — for example applying, enrolling, booking, registering, contacting, obtaining directions, or confirming current details.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The current production site is intentionally lightweight and primarily built with static HTML, CSS, and limited JavaScript.
- A PRASA is independent. It is not part of, and does not act on behalf of, organizations, institutions, providers, or public bodies linked or listed on the site.
- Listings are provided for information and direct access to original sources. Inclusion does not constitute endorsement.
- Information can change. Visitors should confirm current details with the relevant provider before acting.
- Where a listing carries a `Checked [date]` or similar source-status indicator, it means A PRASA reviewed the relevant published source as of that date. It is not a guarantee of continuing availability, eligibility, acceptance, or outcomes.
- Provider, organization, and place identities should be preserved rather than translated or normalized unless an official localized form exists.
- English is currently the canonical editorial language. Portuguese is the first planned controlled localization. Browser translation may remain a fallback but is not the controlled localization system.
- Kriolu localization is deferred. Kriolu names and language already present in authentic local contexts should not be treated as errors.
- Editorial inclusion must remain distinct from any future paid promotion. If paid or sponsored placements are introduced later, they must be visibly identified.
- A PRASA should remain lightweight, understandable, mobile-friendly, and maintainable without unnecessary technical complexity.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: A PRASA.
- `Prasa` is a Kriolu form related to Portuguese `praça`, connecting the identity to the idea of the public square and to Cabo Verdean language and culture.
- The identity is inspired by the broader cross-cultural role of public squares rather than one specific physical square or one particular island.
- Approved brand assets exist under `assets/brand/`.
- Core palette: deep green, ochre, and cream.
- The visual character should feel calm, editorial, human, place-specific, useful, and trustworthy.
- Avoid generic SaaS/startup aesthetics, excessive visual effects, tourism-marketing clichés, aggressive conversion patterns, and unnecessary UI complexity.
- The product should feel like a knowledgeable person pointing someone toward something useful, not a company aggressively trying to sell something.

## Evidence on Hand

The live product contains real, source-led records rather than placeholder listings.

Records may include provider identity, source links, current details, caveats, dates, directions, contact pathways, or source-status information depending on the record type.

Do not fabricate testimonials, reviews, provider claims, prices, dates, availability, eligibility, affiliations, endorsements, or source verification.

When current facts cannot be substantiated from an appropriate source, flag the gap rather than inventing information.

## Product Principles

1. **Useful discovery over exhaustive coverage.**
   Curate intentionally rather than attempting to contain everything.

2. **Independent by design.**
   A PRASA points toward organizations and providers without representing itself as part of them or speaking on their behalf.

3. **Source-led trust.**
   Preserve original provider identity and make it easy for people to continue to the source and confirm current details.

4. **Encounter as well as search.**
   Design should help people discover useful things they did not necessarily arrive knowing to search for.

5. **Calm editorial hierarchy.**
   Prioritize clarity, breathing room, typography, useful structure, and restrained emphasis over visual noise.

6. **One praça, multiple kinds of usefulness.**
   Do not unnecessarily silo residents, visitors, opportunities, culture, practical information, organizations, and community participation into unrelated experiences.

7. **Editorial independence from commerce.**
   Ordinary inclusion must not quietly become pay-to-play. Any future sponsored or paid promotion must be clearly distinguished.

8. **Respect linguistic and local identity.**
   Do not normalize away Portuguese, Kriolu, provider names, place names, or culturally specific language simply to make the site appear uniformly English.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Accessibility is a product requirement, not decorative polish.

Preserve and improve:

- semantic HTML;
- keyboard accessibility;
- visible focus states;
- sufficient text contrast;
- meaningful labels and accessible names;
- responsive layouts without horizontal overflow;
- readable typography and line lengths;
- reduced unnecessary motion;
- usable mobile interaction;
- clear distinction between links, controls, and static content.

Do not sacrifice accessibility for brand styling or visual novelty.
